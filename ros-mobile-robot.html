My contribution

I integrated a Yahboom X3 Mecanum-wheel chassis, an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, a 2D LiDAR, and an Orbbec Astra Pro depth camera. I configured the Linux and ROS 2 environment, built the relevant workspaces, and used an Ubuntu laptop as a remote ground station.

The robot and ground station communicate on the same network with a shared ROS domain. LiDAR scans, camera images, the robot model, and mapping output were checked through ROS topics and RViz.

Integration troubleshooting

Odometry direction disagreed with motion

Symptom
Forward physical motion appeared as negative forward velocity in /vel_raw.
Diagnosis
I inspected velocity feedback directly and traced the sign mismatch to the chassis driver publication logic.
Change
I corrected the velocity signs before publishing the Twist message.
Verification
After restarting the driver, I checked /vel_raw again: forward physical motion produced positive forward odometry.

Serial devices changed between boots

Symptom
The chassis and LiDAR could move between /dev/ttyUSB device names.
Diagnosis
Launch configuration depended on USB enumeration order.
Change
I configured udev aliases using USB vendor and product IDs, and addressed the chassis serial-driver and service conflict.
Verification
I verified the serial devices and used the persistent aliases in driver configuration.

Clock drift affected TF and builds

Symptom
The Jetson could lose correct time after power loss, causing TF issues and clock-skew warnings.
Diagnosis
The system clocks needed synchronization before interpreting TF behavior.
Change
I configured Chrony with an initial clock correction policy.

Ground-station discovery was unreliable

Symptom
WSL2 network discovery caused connection problems in the remote ROS workflow.
Diagnosis
The ground-station environment added a network-discovery problem to hardware debugging.
Change
I switched to a native Ubuntu 22.04 laptop with the same ROS domain as the robot.
Verification
I inspected topics and used RViz from the native Ubuntu ground station.

Motion calibration

I used the vendor's linear and angular calibration nodes to compare commanded motion with physical motion. The linear scale correction used the commanded-to-measured distance ratio. For angular calibration, I compared a commanded full rotation with the actual rotation and updated the scale factor.

Indoor mapping

I first used Gmapping to exercise the chain from chassis motion and odometry through LiDAR and TF to map generation. I also tested Cartographer after observing odometry drift on the Mecanum platform. The comparison explored loop closure when revisiting mapped areas.

I inspected maps in RViz and saved them using the Nav2 map server.

Outcomes

  • Integrated the chassis, LiDAR, and depth camera in ROS 2.
  • Completed teleoperation, odometry debugging and calibration, and indoor mapping.
  • Resolved integration issues across Linux devices, drivers, ROS configuration, and time synchronization.
